PROTOTYPE: Clash board

The board from spec #558: B1's overview with A1's detail of who plays. The open question is #571: how each player's Discord confirmation and Riot's view of the client show side by side.

Riot's API can tell us that a player is on the team in the client. It can't tell us that they readied up, locked in or have a ticket. Each variant below shows only what the API gives. Flip variants with the yellow bar or the arrow keys.

A. Two marks per player

Each player gets two small marks after their name: the first is their confirmation, the second is the client. One legend line explains them.

It's compact and keeps A1's mention lists, but the reader has to learn the legend.

B. Two columns per team

Each team lists its players one per line, with a "Discord" and a "Client" column. It's B1's day grid turned on its side.

It's the easiest to read and needs no legend, but it's the tallest. With four teams, the board passes Discord's 40-component limit sooner, so the rest of the teams move behind a "More teams" button.

Catch: Components V2 has no columns, and Discord's font isn't monospaced. The columns only line up inside a code block, where mentions don't work. Without one, each player needs a section of their own, which uses components even faster.

C. Grouped by what's left to do

Each team's players are sorted by state: ready, confirmed but not in the client, no answer, declined. The line that needs action comes first, so a captain knows whom to chase.

It needs no legend and stays short, but nobody's position in the list stays fixed: players move as they act.
